# QIR Optimizer: Review 3 Specification (as built)

**Status:** implemented · **Updated:** 2026-10-23 · **Course:** BCSE307 Compiler Design
**Team:** Shane (Member 1), Members 2–4 (names to be filled in)

## 1. Claim

A QIR-to-QIR optimizer that reasons across the quantum/classical boundary and checks itself.

1. **C1. Complete compiler.** An OpenQASM 2.0 front-end (lexer, recursive-descent parser with panic-mode and phrase-level recovery, semantic analysis) emits QIR in the Base or Adaptive Profile with opaque pointers. A QIR reader accepts any producer, including legacy typed-pointer modules.
2. **C2. Commutation-aware peephole optimization** along qubit wires, with conservative aliasing for SSA qubits. Every algebraic rule is machine-checked on all gate pairs and placements over three qubits.
3. **C3. Hybrid quantum–classical constant propagation.** SCCP over the CFG, with a product lattice over qubit basis states (⊥ ⊑ |0⟩, |1⟩ ⊑ ⊤), measurement results and SSA constants. It folds `read_result`, `icmp` and `br`, removes unreachable blocks, merges blocks, and lowers programs from the Adaptive to the Base Profile.
4. **C4. Translation validation and a trace.** Every rewrite is checked by exact simulation, enumerating all measurement branches. Each refused rewrite records the blocking dependence (measurement, reset, unknown call, possible alias, non-commuting gate, superposition).

Positioning against RPO (CGO 2021), QCP (SAS 2023), BQCP and MQT hybrid de-quantization (2026), QAT, Qiskit and QIRO is in walkthrough §9. The claim concerns the layer and the integration, not a new cancellation identity or a more precise state domain.

## 3. Evidence (regenerated by `npm run evidence`)

- 65/65 tests:
  - front-end diagnostics;
  - exhaustive rule soundness (over 1,000 commutation claims);
  - property tests (random programs, unitary and program equivalence, translation validation of every step, fuzzing of the front-end);
  - the naive optimizer being caught;
  - LLVM verification of examples and random outputs.
- 22 benchmarks under three configurations (ablation): 48% of 4,286 gates removed (full), against 39% (peephole) and 24% (adjacent pairs, the Review 2 prototype). 19/22 are proven equivalent (3 have too many branches to enumerate), and 22/22 are accepted by LLVM.
- Success metrics are simulated (ESP plus noisy sampling); there are no hardware results.

## 5. Limitations

- Rewriting is local, within basic blocks.
- The quantum domain knows basis states only.
- There is no phase-polynomial merging, so the QFT round trip is only partly cancelled.
- Exact validation reaches about 20 qubits.
- There is no hardware mapping.
- Semantics assume ideal execution.
- Noise results are simulated.
